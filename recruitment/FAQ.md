## Economics & Payment

### Q1: How much can I earn per month?

**A:** You can earn $20-40/month based on reviews completed:
- **2 reviews** = $20
- **3 reviews** = $30
- **4 reviews** = $40

We cap at 4 reviews/month during beta to ensure quality and prevent burnout.

**Long-term:** After we validate the model, we may increase earning potential or offer performance bonuses.

---

### Q2: Why do I have to pay $100/month if I'm earning $20-40?

**A:** Great question. Think of it this way:

**You're paying for two services:**
1. **Author service ($100/month)**: Submit your papers and get fast, expert reviews
2. **Reviewer service (earn $20-40/month)**: Get compensated for reviewing papers

**Net cost:** $60-80/month for a dramatically better peer review experience on both sides.

**Not actively publishing?** We'll offer a reviewer-only tier in Phase 2 (no subscription, just earnings). But for beta, we need to test the full two-sided marketplace.

---

### Q3: What if I don't have any papers to submit?

**A:** A few options:

1. **Wait for Phase 2:** We're planning a reviewer-only tier (target: Q2 2026) with no subscription, just earnings. You'd be first to join.

2. **Collaborate:** If you co-author with colleagues who could use the platform, the subscription could be shared/reimbursed by a lab budget.

3. **Pass for now:** If you're not actively publishing, this beta might not be the right fit. We'd love to have you when the reviewer-only tier launches.

---

### Q4: How do payouts work?

**Payment processing:**
- Via Stripe Connect (bank-level security)

**Payment methods:**

**Minimum payout:** $20 (must complete at least 2 reviews)

**Tax implications:**

### Q6: What if I complete more than 4 reviews in a month?

**Goal:** Sustainable workload, not exhausting grind.

## Time & Workload

### Q7: How long does a peer review take?

**Average:** 1.5-2 hours per review, depending on:
- Paper length
- Complexity
- Your familiarity with topic
- How thorough you want to be

**Monthly time commitment:**
- 2 reviews = 3-4 hours
- 3 reviews = 4.5-6 hours
- 4 reviews = 6-8 hours

**Compare to traditional peer review:** Same time per review, but better tools (saves ~15-30 min) + you're compensated.

**No problem.** You control your review volume.

**Options:**
1. **Decline assignments:** Turn down papers when they're offered (no penalty)

3. **Adjust preferences:** Lower your monthly review frequency in settings (e.g., 2/month instead of 4/month)

**What happens:**

- You just won't have reviewer earnings that month

**Goal:** Flexibility. Life happens. We get it.

## Paper Matching & Quality

### Q11: What papers will I review?

**Only papers matched to your expertise:**
- Based on your Google Scholar profile (publications, keywords, citations)
- Your self-reported specializations
- Your stated preferences (methods, populations, topics)

**Matching algorithm considers:**
- Topic alignment (primary factor)
- Methodological expertise (stats, techniques)
- Recent activity in related areas
- Conflict avoidance (see Q12)

**You can decline any assignment** if it's not a good fit. No penalty.

---

### Q12: How do you prevent conflicts of interest?

**Automatic exclusions:**
- Papers from your institution
- Papers from co-authors (anyone you've published with)
- Papers from close collaborators (based on citation networks)
- Papers you've cited in past 2 years

**Manual exclusions:**
- You can add specific people/institutions to your "never match" list
- You can decline any assignment if you spot a conflict we missed

**Anonymous review option:** Available if preferred (author won't see your name).

---

### Q13: What if I get assigned a paper I'm not qualified to review?

**First: Decline it.** No penalty. We'd rather you decline than submit a weak review.

**Then: Tell us.** This helps us improve the matching algorithm. Reply to the assignment email with:
- Why it wasn't a good fit
- What would have been a better match

**We're learning too.** Beta is about refining the algorithm. Your feedback is gold.

---

### Q14: What if the paper is really bad? Do I have to be nice?

**No.** Be honest and constructive.

**Our reviewer guidelines:**
- **Honest:** Don't inflate quality to be polite
- **Constructive:** Explain how to improve, don't just criticize
- **Professional:** Critique the work, not the person
- **Thorough:** Specific feedback > vague comments

**Bad papers deserve honest reviews.** That's the point of peer review. Just be respectful in delivery.

---

### Q15: What happens if my review is low quality?

**Quality checks:**
- Authors rate reviews (1-5 stars)
- System flags reviews <500 words or with minimal feedback
- Repeated low-quality reviews trigger manual review

**Consequences:**
- First time: Coaching/feedback (we want to help you improve)
- Repeated issues: Reduction in assignments (fewer reviews offered)
- Severe/persistent issues: Removal from platform (rare, last resort)

**Goal:** Maintain high standards without being punitive. We'll work with you first.

## Objections & Concerns

### Q29: Doesn't paying for reviews create bias?

**Valid concern. Here's how we address it:**

**Payment is for time, not for positive reviews:**
- You get paid the same whether you recommend accept or reject
- No bonus for positive reviews (in fact, we may penalize obviously inflated reviews)
- Your professional reputation is on the line (your name is attached, unless you choose anonymous)

**Compare to traditional peer review:**
- Journals pay editors (not reviewers)
- Editors select who reviews (potential bias)
- No transparency into reviewer selection

**Our view:**
- Compensating expertise doesn't inherently create bias
- Clear guidelines and quality control prevent it
- Transparency (author ratings, review history) keeps reviewers accountable

---

### Q30: Is this replacing traditional journals?

**No. We're complementary, not competitive.**

**What we're replacing:**
- Slow email-based workflows
- Free labor model (reviewers get paid $0)
- Opaque reviewer matching (no one knows how editors select reviewers)
- Terrible tools (PDF annotation, email chains)

**What we're not replacing:**
- Academic journals (we don't publish papers)
- Editorial oversight (authors still submit to journals)
- Journal prestige (Nature, Science, etc. still matter)

**Think of us as:**
- A review platform, not a publisher
- A marketplace connecting authors and reviewers
- A modernization of the peer review *process*

**Long-term vision:** Journals could integrate our platform (some might). But that's Phase 5+.
