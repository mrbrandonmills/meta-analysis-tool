## Response to Common Questions

### Q: "Why would I pay $100 to earn $40?"

**Response:**
Great question! Two perspectives:

**1. You're already submitting papers:**
The $100/month gets you fast, expert reviews on your papers. The $20-40 you earn reviewing offsets the cost. Net: $60-80/month for a dramatically better experience.

**2. Reviewer-only mode (Phase 2):**
If you only want to review (not submit papers), we'll have a reviewer-only tier with no subscription. But for beta, we need the full two-sided marketplace to test properly.

**Bottom line:** This is for active researchers who both submit AND review papers - which describes most people in academia.

Does that make sense?

---

### Q: "How much time does this take?"

**Response:**
Time breakdown:
- Onboarding: 15 minutes (one-time)
- Per review: 1.5-2 hours on average
- Monthly: 3-4 hours for 2 reviews, 6-8 hours for 4 reviews

You control your review volume. Start with 2/month, scale up if you like it.

Compare to traditional peer review:
- Same time per review (1.5-2 hours)
- But now you're compensated
- Better tools (saves time vs. PDF annotation)
- Only relevant papers (no wasted time on mismatches)

Net: Similar time, better experience, actual compensation.

---

### Q: "What's the catch?"

**Response:**
No catch! Just transparency about the trade-offs:

**Pros:**
- Get paid for reviews (finally)
- Modern tools and workflow
- AI matching (only relevant papers)
- Faster reviews on your papers

**Cons:**
- $100/month subscription (but you earn $20-40 back)
- Beta version (bugs possible, features evolving)
- Smaller paper pool initially (10 beta testers)
- Time commitment (2-4 reviews/month)

This isn't magic - it's a better economic model for peer review. You're trading a subscription fee for better service + reviewer compensation.

If you're publishing papers anyway, the value is clear. If you're not actively submitting, this might not be for you (yet - see Phase 2 reviewer-only tier).

## Personalization Templates

### How to Personalize Each Message

**Step 1: Research the person (5 minutes)**
- Read their most recent paper (at least the abstract)
- Check their Google Scholar profile
- Note their specialty area
- Find their institutional affiliation
- Check for any social media presence

**Step 2: Fill in the blanks**
- [First Name]: Their first name
- [Last Name]: Their last name
- [specific paper/topic]: Title or topic of recent paper
- [specific contribution]: What was novel/interesting about it
- [research area]: Their main specialty (e.g., "memory and false memories")
- [Institution]: Where they work
- [Journal]: Where they recently published

**Step 3: Add specific hooks**
- "I noticed your recent work on [X]" → Shows you did research
- "Your 2024 paper on [Y] was interesting because [Z]" → Shows you read their work
- "Given your expertise in [area], you'd be perfect for reviewing papers on [related area]" → Shows relevance

**Step 4: Match tone to channel**
- LinkedIn: Professional but conversational
- Twitter: Casual, brief, friendly
- Email: Formal to semi-formal depending on seniority
- Adjust formality based on their online presence

**Day 1:** Send personalized cold email (Version A, B, or C)

## Best Practices

### Do's:
- Personalize every message (mention their work)
- Keep it brief (busy people, busy inboxes)
- Lead with value, not features
- Make responding easy (yes/no questions)
- Follow up 2-3 times (persistence pays)
- Track everything (spreadsheet/CRM)
- A/B test different approaches
- Be genuinely respectful of their time

### Don'ts:
- Don't spam (max 3 touches per channel)
- Don't use generic templates without personalization
- Don't oversell or hype
- Don't be pushy or aggressive
- Don't contact on multiple channels simultaneously (feels spammy)
- Don't give up after one no-response (follow up!)
- Don't forget to thank people for their time

## Response Rate Expectations

**Realistic benchmarks:**
- Cold email response rate: 5-15%
- LinkedIn response rate: 10-20% (if connected)
- Twitter response rate: 5-10%
- Warm intro response rate: 40-60%

**To get 10 beta testers:**
- Contact 50-100 researchers
- Expect 5-15 expressions of interest
- Convert 10 to onboarding completion

**Improve response rates:**
- Strong personalization (+5-10%)
- Referral/warm intro (+20-30%)
- Multiple touchpoints (+5-10%)
- Urgency/scarcity (+5%)
- Clear value proposition (+10%)
