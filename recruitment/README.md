## Key Messages (Memorize These)

### Elevator Pitch (30 seconds):
"We're building a peer review platform that pays reviewers $20-40/month, uses AI to match papers to specialists, and provides modern collaboration tools. Authors pay $100/month subscription, reviewers earn $20-40/month. Net cost: $60-80/month for a dramatically better experience. We need 10 psychology researchers to test it."

### Value Proposition (3 bullets):
1. **Fair compensation:** Finally get paid for peer reviews ($20-40/month)
2. **Smart matching:** AI only matches papers in your specialty (no more random requests)
3. **Modern tools:** Real-time collaboration replaces email/PDF chaos

### Objection Handling (Top 3):

**Objection 1:** "Why would I pay $100 to earn $40?"
**Response:** "You're paying $100 for fast expert reviews on your papers + earning $40 reviewing others. Net cost: $60/month for dramatically better peer review on both sides."

**Objection 2:** "I don't have time."
**Response:** "You're already doing peer reviews, just for free and with worse tools. Same time commitment (2-4 reviews/month), but now compensated and less frustrating."

**Objection 3:** "What if I don't publish much?"
**Response:** "Phase 2 will have reviewer-only tier (no subscription, just earnings). For beta, we need the full ecosystem. But you'd be first in line for reviewer-only when it launches."

## Communication Tone Guidelines

### Be:
- **Professional but approachable** (not stuffy, not overly casual)
- **Enthusiastic but not hype-y** (excited, not salesy)
- **Honest about trade-offs** (beta means bugs, etc.)
- **Respectful of their time** (brief emails, easy responses)
- **Data-driven** (use numbers, be specific)

### Don't be:
- **Pushy or aggressive** (no hard selling)
- **Vague or ambiguous** (be clear about model and expectations)
- **Overpromising** (don't guarantee success)
- **Dismissive of concerns** (validate objections, address thoughtfully)
- **Impersonal** (always personalize, mention their work)

## Red Flags to Watch For

### During Recruitment:
- Researcher has no publications in past 3 years
- No Google Scholar profile (can't test AI enrichment)
- Unprofessional communication
- Asking for free access or special deals
- No institutional affiliation (fraud risk)

### During Onboarding:
- Can't complete profile (stuck for >7 days)
- Payment fails repeatedly (card issues)
- Unresponsive to support emails
- Expresses buyer's remorse immediately
- Aggressive or demanding tone

### During Beta:
- Never completes first review (even with extensions)
- Multiple declined assignments (not engaged)
- Low-quality reviews (minimal feedback)
- Hostile feedback (unconstructive criticism)
- Requests refund after first month (didn't understand model)

**Action:** Address issues early with personal outreach. Some are solvable (technical issues, misunderstandings), others aren't (bad fit, unengaged user).

## Success Signals

### During Recruitment:
- Fast response (<24 hours)
- Thoughtful questions (shows engagement)
- Mentions their own frustrations with peer review (validates problem)
- Expresses enthusiasm about concept
- Offers to refer colleagues

### During Onboarding:
- Completes profile quickly (<48 hours)
- Detailed profile (doesn't skip optional fields)
- Responsive to emails
- Joins Slack/Discord community immediately
- Asks product questions (shows interest)

### During Beta:
- Completes first review early (before deadline)
- High-quality review (detailed, constructive)
- Provides unsolicited product feedback
- Refers colleagues
- Active in community (Slack discussions, office hours)

**Action:** Celebrate these users! They're your champions. Feature them (with permission), ask for testimonials, prioritize their feedback.

## Frequently Asked Questions (Meta-FAQ)

### Q: Which email template should I use?
**A:** Start with Version A (Direct) for most targets. Use Version B (Story-Based) for senior researchers. Use Version C (Question-Based) for known names. Test and see what works best.

### Q: Should I contact everyone at once or in batches?
**A:** Batches of 10-15 per day. Allows you to iterate messaging based on early responses.

### Q: What if I get more than 10 applications?
**A:** Use SELECTION_CRITERIA.md to score and rank. Select top 10 + 2 alternates. Waitlist the rest.

### Q: What if I get fewer than 10 applications?
**A:** Extend outreach to more researchers, increase incentives, broaden criteria (H-index 5+ instead of 10+), or delay launch until you have enough.

### Q: How do I know if someone is interested but just slow to respond?
**A:** Follow up 2-3 times over 10-14 days. After that, assume not interested and move on.

### Q: Should I call researchers or just email?
**A:** Email first. Offer calls for those who express interest. Cold calling academics is generally not effective.

### Q: What if a famous researcher wants to join but conditions apply?
**A:** Case-by-case basis. If they want free access, probably no (breaks the model). If they want flexibility on deadlines, maybe yes. Consult SELECTION_CRITERIA.md for special cases.
