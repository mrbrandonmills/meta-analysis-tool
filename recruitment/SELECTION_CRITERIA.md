# Selection Criteria for Beta Testers

## Overview
If we receive more than 10 applications, we need clear, objective criteria to select the best candidates. This document outlines priority criteria, scoring methodology, and decision-making process.

---

## Priority Criteria (Ranked)

### 1. Research Quality & Impact (30 points)

**H-Index (20 points)**
- 20+ H-index: 20 points
- 15-19 H-index: 15 points
- 10-14 H-index: 10 points
- 5-9 H-index: 5 points
- <5 H-index: 2 points

**Recent Publications (10 points)**
- 5+ papers in 2023-2024: 10 points
- 3-4 papers in 2023-2024: 7 points
- 1-2 papers in 2023-2024: 4 points
- 0 papers in 2023-2024: 0 points

**Why this matters:**
- Established researchers = credible beta testers
- Active researchers = need peer review regularly
- High H-index = likely strong reviewer themselves

---

### 2. Specialty Diversity (25 points)

**Goal:** 2 researchers from each of 5 specialties

**Scoring:**
- First applicant in specialty: 25 points
- Second applicant in specialty: 20 points
- Third applicant in specialty: 10 points
- Fourth+ applicant in specialty: 5 points

**Specialties:**
- Cognitive psychology
- Clinical psychology
- Social psychology
- Developmental psychology
- Neuroscience/Cognitive neuroscience

**Why this matters:**
- Need diversity to test cross-specialty matching
- Broader appeal in later marketing
- Different research cultures/needs

---

### 3. Google Scholar Profile Quality (15 points)

**Profile completeness (10 points)**
- Complete profile (photo, bio, all papers): 10 points
- Mostly complete (some missing info): 6 points
- Basic profile (just papers listed): 3 points
- Incomplete or absent: 0 points

**Profile recency (5 points)**
- Updated within last 3 months: 5 points
- Updated within last year: 3 points
- Not updated in 1+ years: 0 points

**Why this matters:**
- AI enrichment depends on good Scholar data
- Shows tech-savviness and online presence
- Active profiles = active researchers

---

### 4. Responsiveness & Communication (10 points)

**Response speed (5 points)**
- Replied within 24 hours: 5 points
- Replied within 3 days: 3 points
- Replied after 3+ days: 1 point

**Communication quality (5 points)**
- Thoughtful, detailed response: 5 points
- Brief but professional: 3 points
- Very brief or unclear: 1 point

**Why this matters:**
- Fast responders = engaged users
- Good communicators = good reviewers
- Predictor of onboarding completion

---

### 5. Institutional Diversity (10 points)

**Goal:** Avoid clustering all beta testers at 1-2 universities

**Scoring:**
- First applicant from institution: 10 points
- Second applicant from institution: 5 points
- Third+ applicant from institution: 2 points

**Geographic diversity bonus:**
- International (non-US): +3 points
- Different US regions represented: +2 points

**Why this matters:**
- Avoid echo chamber
- Broader network for future recruitment
- Different institutional cultures/practices

---

### 6. Peer Review Experience (5 points)

**Self-reported review volume (if asked)**
- 15+ reviews in past year: 5 points
- 10-14 reviews: 4 points
- 5-9 reviews: 3 points
- 1-4 reviews: 1 point
- Unsure/none: 0 points

**Why this matters:**
- Experience with peer review = understands pain points
- High volume = comfortable with commitment

---

### 7. Technical Aptitude (5 points)

**Signals:**
- Uses ORCID (automatic): +2 points
- Active on Twitter/ResearchGate: +2 points
- Has personal/lab website: +1 point
- Mentions interest in AI/tech in response: +2 points

**Why this matters:**
- Comfort with new platforms
- Ability to provide technical feedback
- Less onboarding friction

---

## Total Score: 100 points

**Selection Process:**
1. Score all applicants on 0-100 scale
2. Rank by score
3. Apply diversity filters (see below)
4. Select top 10 (or 12 if there are ties)

---

## Diversity Filters (Must-Have)

Even if someone scores highly, we must ensure:

### Specialty Balance
- Minimum 1, maximum 3 from each specialty
- Ideal: 2 from each specialty

### Gender Balance
- Aim for 40-60% split (if data available)
- Don't ask directly, infer from names/profiles if possible

### Career Stage Balance
- Mix of junior (assistant professors, postdocs) and senior (associate/full professors)
- Ideal: 4 early-career, 4 mid-career, 2 senior

### Institution Tier Balance
- Mix of R1, R2, and teaching-focused institutions
- Not all from "elite" universities (Harvard, Stanford, MIT)

### Geographic Balance
- At least 7 US states represented
- At least 1 international researcher (if applications received)

---

## Red Flags (Automatic Rejection or Lower Priority)

### Disqualifiers:
- H-index <3 (insufficient research record)
- No publications in past 3 years (inactive researcher)
- No Google Scholar profile (can't test AI enrichment)
- Rude or unprofessional communication
- No institutional affiliation (fraud risk)
- Asked for free access or special deals (misaligned expectations)

### Yellow Flags (Proceed with Caution):
- Very slow to respond (>7 days)
- Unclear research specialty
- No ORCID (not a dealbreaker, but less ideal)
- Institution outside top 100 (verify legitimacy)
- Very junior (grad student) - prefer postdoc+ for beta

---

## Tiebreaker Criteria

If two candidates have identical scores, use these tiebreakers:

1. **Specialty need** - Pick the one filling an underrepresented specialty
2. **Referral** - Pick the one who was referred by someone we trust
3. **Enthusiasm** - Pick the one who expressed more interest/excitement
4. **Timeline** - Pick the one who applied earlier (reward promptness)
5. **Fit** - Subjective judgment: Who seems like a better fit for collaborative beta?

## Selection Committee (If Applicable)

If you're making decisions with a team:

### Roles:
- **Founder/CEO**: Final decision-maker
- **CTO**: Evaluates technical aptitude and profile quality
- **Academic Advisor**: Evaluates research quality and reputation
- **Product Lead**: Evaluates fit for beta testing and feedback quality

### Process:
1. Each committee member scores independently
2. Meet to discuss discrepancies
3. Reach consensus on top 10 (+2 alternates)
4. Founder makes final call on any ties

---

## Decision Timeline

### Day 1-3: Applications Open
- Promote to target list
- Monitor applications as they come in
- Quick-scan for obvious disqualifiers

### Day 4-7: Scoring Period
- Score all applicants using rubric
- Check for diversity balance
- Identify top candidates and borderline cases

### Day 8: Selection Meeting (if team)
- Review scores
- Discuss borderline cases
- Make final selections

### Day 9: Notifications Sent
- Send acceptance emails to top 10 (+2 alternates)
- Send waitlist emails to strong candidates not selected
- Send rejection emails (polite, encouraging)

### Day 10-14: Confirmations & Onboarding
- Give accepted candidates 5 days to confirm
- If anyone declines, pull from alternates
- Begin onboarding once 10 confirmed

## Special Cases

### What if a famous researcher applies late?

**Scenario:** Dr. Susan Fiske (H-index 150+) applies on Day 10, but we've already selected our 10.

**Decision criteria:**
- Is their value > the value of our 10th-ranked selection?
- Would their name/reputation significantly boost credibility?
- Are they willing to commit to full participation (not just lending their name)?

**Options:**
1. **Expand to 11-12 testers** (if budget allows)
2. **Replace lowest-ranked selection** (if specialty overlap exists)
3. **Defer to next cohort with priority status** (honor existing commitments)

**Recommendation:** Option 1 or 3. Don't burn bridges with already-selected testers.

---

### What if we don't get 10 applications?

**If <10 applications in first week:**

**Immediate actions:**
1. Extend outreach to next 20 researchers on target list
2. Increase visibility (social media, relevant subreddits, email lists)
3. Ask existing applicants for referrals
4. Offer higher incentives (60% off first month instead of 50%?)

**Longer-term solutions:**
1. Broaden criteria (H-index 5+ instead of 10+)
2. Expand to adjacent fields (education psychology, neuroscience)
3. Accept international researchers (if previously US-focused)
4. Lower barriers (shorter onboarding, simpler requirements)

**Don't:**
- Accept unqualified researchers (damages proof of concept)
- Launch with <5 testers (too small to test matching)
- Rush the process (better to delay than accept wrong people)

**Minimum viable:** 6-8 testers across at least 4 specialties.

---

### What if someone applies who knows the founder personally?

**Potential bias:** Personal relationships cloud judgment.

**Mitigation:**
1. Disclose the relationship to any selection committee
2. Have someone else score that application
3. Apply strict scoring criteria (no favoritism)
4. If borderline, err toward waitlist (avoid appearance of nepotism)

**Golden rule:** Would you be comfortable if this decision was public? If not, reconsider.

---

## Post-Selection: Monitoring & Adjustment

### Week 1 After Selection
- Track onboarding completion rate
- Identify any drop-offs early
- Pull from waitlist if needed

### Week 2-4
- Monitor engagement (logins, profile completion)
- Reach out to any silent/struggling testers
- Celebrate early completions (first review done!)

### Month 2-3
- Assess if all 10 are active
- Consider adding from waitlist if some are inactive
- Don't be afraid to part ways with non-engaged testers

**Success criteria:**
- 8/10 complete onboarding within 2 weeks
- 6/10 complete first review within 4 weeks
- 5/10 still active at month 3

If not hitting these, revisit selection criteria for next cohort.

---

## Continuous Improvement

### After Beta Cohort 1, Review:

**What worked:**
- Which criteria predicted success? (high correlation between score and engagement)
- Which criteria didn't matter? (maybe H-index wasn't predictive after all)
- What surprised us? (unexpected patterns)

**What to change for Cohort 2:**
- Adjust scoring weights (e.g., increase weight on responsiveness if that predicted success)
- Add new criteria (e.g., social media presence, if that mattered)
- Remove criteria (e.g., institutional tier, if it didn't matter)

**Data to track:**
- Application score vs. onboarding completion
- Application score vs. review completion rate
- Application score vs. quality ratings
- Diversity metrics vs. user satisfaction

**Goal:** Refine selection process to maximize engaged, high-quality beta testers in future cohorts.

---

## Summary: Selection Philosophy

**Core principle:** We're looking for engaged, qualified researchers who will:
1. Complete onboarding and use the platform regularly
2. Provide thoughtful, constructive feedback
3. Represent diverse perspectives and specialties
4. Help us prove the concept to future users

**Balanced priorities:**
- **Quality** (research credentials, experience)
- **Diversity** (specialty, institution, geography, career stage)
- **Engagement** (responsiveness, communication, enthusiasm)
- **Fit** (technical aptitude, alignment with mission)

**When in doubt:**
- Choose the more engaged applicant over the more credentialed
- Choose the applicant filling a diversity gap
- Choose the applicant who will provide better feedback
- Choose the applicant who seems most excited

**Remember:** We're not just selecting users. We're selecting collaborators who will help us build this platform. Choose wisely.
